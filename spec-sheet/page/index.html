What the numbers say

  • Hover: lean the fan about 20 deg and the arm holds at 0.18 of stall

    With the fan kept upright (within 3 deg) the best pose found puts 0.67 of stall on joint 2 at hover and 1.42 at full thrust: over your 2x rule, and joint 2 overheats. Let the fan lean off vertical (the eight arms' sideways pushes cancel) and thrust starts carrying the arm: 10 deg gives 0.38 for 1.5 % of lift, 20 deg gives 0.18 at hover and 0.37 at full thrust for 6 % of lift.

  • It cannot hold itself out straight with the fan off

    Straight and level, joint 2 needs 1.30 of stall (the helix arm's worst is 1.10). Arched up with the tip held level, it reaches 0.95 m from the root under stall and 0.50 m under your 2x rule. Carrying anything held out needs the fan's help or a short reach.

  • Walking on the folded pod: feet need to sit about 45 cm out

    With the feet 30 cm from the hub axis the outer joints (7 to 9, STS3215) run at 1.28 of stall, and 2.75 at twice the foot load. At 35 cm the worst is 0.69; at 45 cm it drops to 0.48, inside your 2x rule. The fold that brings a 1 m arm down to a near foot puts the foot load across the outer joints. 25 cm was out of reach under these clearance rules for both arms.

  • The horn screws are the loosest link

    Four M3 screws through 3.4 mm holes in PETG hold 7.0 N.m by friction when new and about 3.5 N.m once the plastic relaxes, which is under an STS3250's 4.9 N.m stall. When it slips, the hole clearance adds up to 1.64 deg of play at that joint. Ream to 3.1 mm and add washers, or bond the ear to the horn.

  • Tip play: 9 mm typical, 35 mm worst case as designed; 4 times that if the horn clamps slip

    Servo backlash (0.5 deg each, assumed) is 90 % of it. The 8.4 mm printed idler bores add half again; reamed to 8.05 to 8.10 mm they add almost nothing. Gravity and thrust usually hold every joint against one side of its play, so the tip only wanders this much when the load reverses.

  • Root gear contact stress is the long-run limit at joint 0

    At stall the m1 7075 pair sees 547 MPa contact and 155 MPa root bending, fine for peaks; at hover it sees 373 MPa. Anodized aluminium pits under long running above roughly 350 MPa. A steel pinion removes the question.

  • Weakest printed part: joint 0 idler ear, safety factor 2.1

    Across every solved hover, tilt, full-thrust and 2x walking load, with PETG at 25 MPa across the layers. Everything clears your 2x rule.

  • Servo heat decides the hover pose

    With the fan upright, joint 0 reaches a 50 K rise in 5.5 min, joint 2 reaches a 50 K rise in 2.7 min, joint 4 reaches a 50 K rise in 16.4 min, against a 17 minute flight, and the arm's servos burn 72.8 W. In the 20 deg leaning pose the arm burns 23.4 W and no servo overheats in a flight. A holding servo turns all its input into heat; about 3 W per case is the steady limit (assumed), roughly a fifth of stall torque.

  • Landing: it has to land under power

    Dropped 5 cm onto seven folded-pod feet with the servos as the only spring, each foot takes about 86 N and the worst joint sees 3.6 times stall, enough to back-drive or strip a gear train. Let thrust carry the weight to touchdown and hand it over slowly, or add a soft foot pad.

Configuration and method

  • Arm: 1.00 m, 13 pin-hinge joints at 76.9 mm pitch, 57.1 mm outer diameter. Hinge axes turn 90 deg from one joint to the next, so joints alternate pitch (even: 0, 2 … 12) and yaw (odd). That is the closest no-helix layout to the helix arm: same parts, one vertebra STL for every moving link, and pitch and yaw stay separated.
  • Servos: STS3250 (4.9 N.m stall, 12 V) in joints 0 to 4, STS3215 (2.94 N.m) in joints 5 to 12, a 2:1 m1 7075 spur stage on joint 0 (9.8 N.m). 6705 support bearings on the horn side of joints 1 to 4, 7075 idler pins on 0 to 4, printed 8 mm PETG stubs on 5 to 12.
  • Tip: folding fan pod, 252.5 g, MN5008 KV400 with a 16x5.4 prop 102 mm out; thrust acts along the tip axis.
  • Robot: 19.15 kg with the claw-fan wiring. Hover split: 21.53 N per claw fan and 16.2 N from the central prop, about 2820 W.
  • Method: hand calculations and a rigid-body model of the arm built from the CAD masses (no FEA). Poses are found by optimisation: each one minimises the worst joint's share of stall under its geometric constraints, from many random starts, so a lower-torque pose may still exist. Tolerances use a 20,000-sample Monte Carlo. Values marked [A] are assumptions; the full list is at the end.

Model, scripts and raw results: octopus-drone/spec-sheet in the project files (spec_lib.py, sec1 to sec5, results/). CAD regenerated without the helix: spec-sheet/cad_alt90.

Servo bus voltage sagsThe upright hover pose holds down to 8.1 V (your 2x rule would need 16.1 V, above the 12 V supply); the leaning pose holds down to 2.2 V (4.4 V for 2x). Stall torque falls in proportion to voltage. The STS servos cut out near 4 to 5 V by default [A]. Feed each arm's 12 V bus from its own buck regulator (about 10 A peak per arm) so one arm's stall cannot pull the others down; the pack sags from 25 V to about 21 V under hover load, which a buck converter absorbs.
Overload and back-driveFeetech STS servos have torque limit, protection current and overload protection time registers. In flight a servo that trips goes limp, which is worse than running hot, so for joints 0 to 4 set the overload torque to 100 % and the protection time to its maximum, and let the flight controller's own current watch decide. For walking, a 80 % torque limit makes the arm give way before the gears strip. Metal gear trains in this size back-drive around 1.5 x stall [A]; above that teeth chip.

A dead servo leaves its joint free (no brake). The other joints move so that thrust and gravity put no torque on the free joint, with the fan still within 20 deg of up and 0.6 m out. If that fails the arm should cut its fan and fold (neutral thrust).

Assumptions [A]

  1. Servo stall torque and current are the makers' 12 V figures; servo backlash 0.5 deg; hold stiffness = stall torque per 2 deg of error.
  2. Metal gear trains back-drive or chip at about 1.5 x stall.
  3. Printed PETG: 45 MPa along layers, 25 MPa across, 35 MPa bearing, E 2.0 GPa, fatigue 30 % of strength at a million cycles. Prints vary; test a coupon.
  4. 7075-T6: yield 503 MPa, bending fatigue 159 MPa, Basquin 1466 MPa and -0.143.
  5. Servo case sheds about 3 W steadily in still air; 50 K rise allowed.
  6. Hub radius 124.5 mm (the smallest at which the eight root flanges clear); mantle not drawn.
  7. Tip pod 252.5 g with its CG 45 mm beyond the tip plate; real claw mass unknown, swept 150 to 400 g.
  8. Horn screws at 0.5 N.m, friction 0.3, PETG relaxes half the clamp.
  9. Root gear: m1, 20T / 40T, 10 mm face, 98 % efficient; centre held ±0.02 mm only with reamed seats.
  10. Fan thrust and power from the maker's MN5008 16x5.4 table, 5 % derated at 6S under load.
  11. No FEA: ears, pins and bores use beam and bearing formulas with the support reactions split over the two ears 49 mm apart.
