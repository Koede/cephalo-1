Cephalo-1 Joint Study

The study was rerun with 1 m arms: 94,048 arm designs from 101 joint types. The main design is now the pin hinge in a 60° helix, 1 m long with 13 joints, STS3250s in joints 0 to 4 and claw fans of about 16 N: an 18.8 mm shape miss. Joint 0 drives through a 2:1 gear, which takes the lift-test miss from 345 mm to 5.9 mm; the robot is 15.08 kg with a thrust-to-weight ratio of 1.150. On today's 10.8 N fans only 7-joint arms fly at 1 m.

Main design: 1 m arm, 13 joints, 16 N claw fans, geared root

The main design is the pin hinge in a 60° helix, 1 m long with 13 joints: STS3250 servos in joints 0 to 4 and STS3215 beyond, one 31 g vertebra printed twelve times per arm, collision-free to ±90° per joint. Root fix: 2:1 gear on joint 0

The 345 mm lift-test miss came from the start of the test: the arm begins straight out and level with the fans off and is commanded to the walking stance in one jump. Straight out, gravity needs 7.2 N·m at joint 0 against the STS3250's 4.9 N·m, so the arm folds and never reaches the stance. Started from the stance, the same arm lifts its foot to 13 mm. The adopted fix drives joint 0 through a 2:1 spur stage (9.8 N·m, 2.2 rad/s at the joint) and keeps every command under 80 % of the servo's rated speed. 36 variants were run; the main ones are below.

What the run found

  • (a) 1 m on today's thrust: 7 joints, STS3250 in joints 0 to 2. 142.9 mm pitch, one 73 g vertebra printed six times per arm, 920 g per arm outside the mantle. Robot 11.38 kg, thrust-to-weight 1.15, right on the gate. 60 mm shape miss. Wave tip error 20 mm at 0.25 Hz and 97 mm at 0.5 Hz; it saturates at 1 Hz.
  • (b) Main design. 1 m with 16 N claw fans: 13 joints, STS3250 in joints 0 to 4, 2:1 gear on joint 0. 76.9 mm pitch, one 31 g vertebra, 1,243 g per arm outside the mantle before the gear stage. Robot 15.08 kg, thrust-to-weight 1.150. 18.8 mm shape miss. Wave error 22 mm at 0.25 Hz and 62 mm at 0.5 Hz; 5.9 mm in the lift test.
  • Root fix: straight out with the fans off, joint 0 needs 7.2 N·m against the STS3250's 4.9 N·m, so the arm folded at the start of the lift test (345 mm miss); from the stance it lifted to 13 mm. A 2:1 spur stage on joint 0 (+46.2 g per arm, now drawn into the root socket) with every move kept under 80 % of servo speed brings the lift to 5.9 mm, the curl step from 386 to 86 mm and the 0.5 Hz wave from 106 to 62 mm. Bigger servos and a second STS3250 fail the thrust-to-weight gate; a torsion spring fights the servo; a 4 N claw-fan assist while the arm rises fixes only the lift (14 mm). The redrawn socket keeps every vertebra as it was; the servo sits 30 mm below the hinge and stands 6.8 mm out of the tube on one side, so the mantle opening needs a notch there. Root bearing check: with the claw fan pushing across the hinge plane, the first socket's 6800 was loaded to 1.4 times its static rating and its printed idler stub to about PETG's limit; a 61803 under the gear mesh, an 8 mm 7075 idler pin and a 695 on the pinion bring the worst case to 0.76 of the bearing's static rating (0.85 at full throttle) for 0.9 g more, still inside the budget. Joints 1 to 4 were checked next (below).
  • Joints 1 to 4 side loads: the printed ears pass in every case, and the printed stub passes Forest's 2x rule only while each claw fan pushes within 30° of straight up (12.3 MPa against 12.5). The weak part is the STS3250's own output: the horn side carries 110 N in hover in any pose, 190 to 290 N with the fan within 30° of up and up to 510 N pushed sideways, about 9 mm out from the case. Feetech publishes no limit; ROBOTIS rates its heavier XM430 at 40 N. The fix that takes the load off the servo is a thin-section bearing between the fork's horn ear and the cradle lid at joints 1 to 4, roughly +20 to +40 g per arm (T/W about 1.13); a 0 g alternative is to bench-test an STS3250 under 110 and 300 N first. Now drawn: a 6705 thin-section bearing (25 x 32 x 4 mm, 834 N static) on the servo lid round the horn, inside the 57.1 mm tube, 32 g per arm, T/W 1.061 to 1.045; joints still swing +-90 deg clear. It carries the worst case at 0.61 of its static rating.
  • Central prop (replaces the EDF): under the dome wins. A 17 inch prop on a T-Motor MN5212 KV420 (70 A class ESC) sits 125 mm under the hub centre; above the dome a 22 inch MN6007II would need a mast about 190 mm tall to clear the arms when they arch up while walking. The 22 inch is more efficient per watt, but above the dome the fast slipstream hits the dome and the eight arm roots and pushes down with about 22 % of its thrust, against about 4 % under it, so hover power comes out the same (about 3.5 kW with a 1 kg pack, against 5.6 kW with the EDF). Hover with the central prop under the dome: 1 kg pack 2.6 min at T/W 1.13, 1.6 kg 4.0 min at 1.09, 2.3 kg 5.4 min at 1.04 (the EDF gave 1.6, 2.6 and 3.5 min). Above the dome cannot hover the 2.3 kg pack inside the claw fans' rating. The cost of under: blade tips 75 mm off the ground while walking and 22 mm from the legs in stance 4, so a ring guard (about 0.1 kg) is worth adding. Pack: Forest picked 2.3 kg. No 6S LiPo weighs that; the design now uses a Tattu 6S 16,000 mAh 30C (1.99 kg): 4.5 min hover at T/W 1.06, 181 A against its 480 A. The 2.65 kg Tattu 22,000 mAh gives 5.8 min at T/W 1.02. Two DOGCOM semi-solid 22,000 mAh packs (2.78 kg) would give about 11.5 min, but at T/W 1.01 and 88 % of their 220 A continuous rating.
  • Claw prop clearance while walking: with a foot down, no fan direction within 30° of straight up is clear: the arm's own last links come down onto the claw and sit in the prop disc. The claw fans stay stopped while their foot is down, the wrist pivot needs about 120 mm of ground clearance for a stopped 7 inch prop (unless the props fold), and at take-off each claw tips 40 to 60° back toward the body or waits until it is clear.
  • Mantle ports: cutters and a template for the new 61 x 42 mm opening with the notch for the root servo sleeve are in arm-pin-hinge/out_1m_13 (mantle_port_*). Two root flange holes moved from 30° and 330° to 45° and 315° to clear it, at no mass change. At the study's assumed 110 mm hub radius the eight root flanges overlap each other; they clear from 124.5 mm.
  • Claw pickups: pickups are by the claw only. From the walking stance the arm lifts 0.4 to 0.6 kg at Forest's 2x rule; reached out straight it can lift almost nothing extra.
  • Still unchecked: the real claw mass. The sim allows 130 g at the tip; the 2807 motor alone is 53 g, and the claw, wrist, ESC and prop are not designed or weighed yet.
  • Claw fans for the main design: 16 N each, 13.2 N continuous at hover with the root gear, from a 7 to 7.5 inch prop on a 2807 motor at 6S. About 270 W per fan at hover and 2.2 kW for all eight, before the EDF. A 5 inch FPV motor peaks near 15 N and is too small.
  • (c) 700 mm on today's thrust: 9 joints, STS3250 in joints 0 to 3. 77.8 mm pitch, one 32 g vertebra, 854 g per arm outside the mantle. Robot 11.36 kg, thrust-to-weight 1.15. 30.7 mm shape miss, 27 mm wave error at 1 Hz and 6 mm in the lift test.
  • Length sweep on today's thrust: 9 joints fly up to 800 mm (on all STS3215 at 800 mm, thrust-to-weight 1.18); 900 mm and 1 m fly only with 7 joints. A 1 m arm needs about 4 % more thrust for 9 joints, 19 % for 13 and 31 % for 16.
  • Speed: a 1 m arm moves about four times slower than the 585 mm arm. The root torque scales with length squared at fixed arm mass per metre, and every 1 m design saturates in the 1 Hz wave test. Flight-stress torque at the root is 1.6 to 3.1 times STS3215 stall at 1 m.
  • Architectures: tendons, graded chains and push-pull rods have no feasible design at 1 m on today's thrust, even with low-friction tendons. With 16 N claw fans, 2,391 designs pass, 72 of them graded chains, and the best 11-joint arms with two roll joints reach 13 DOF.
  • Correction to the 585 mm results: two-axis joints (universal and omni-wrist) were charged one servo per joint for mass and size while being given two drives. The 585 mm figures for them ("22 DOF most DOF", "universal 15 DOF 8.82 kg") were too light and are withdrawn. The fix is in the 1 m run; the pin-hinge figures were not affected.
  • Bigger claw fans (Forest, Nov 9): an MN5008 KV400 with a 16x5.4 carbon 2-blade prop (25 N rated, 33.5 N full, +107 g per claw) and two DOGCOM 22 Ah semi-solid packs hover for 17 min at T/W 1.35 (16 min and 1.29 with the bent claw), against 4.4 min today. The 9 to 10.5 inch FPV props gain nothing; the big slow props do. The arm then needs 8 mm 7075 idler pins on joints 1 to 4, the fan held within 60° of up (horn bearing 0.46 of its rating), and 4 to 5 N of fan lift when reaching straight out (joint 3 would be at 1.06 of stall). The 16 inch disc rules out the pan-tilt wrist, so the fan is fixed on the claw axis and the claw bends round the prop edge: out 233 mm behind the prop, a fixed 90° bend, jaws 90 mm past it. The jaws can't be the foot (2.1 times the tip servo's stall), so walking moves onto the arm a little back from the claw. Separately, the stance poses already ask 1.34 and 1.20 of stall from joints 6 and 9.
  • Folding fan pod (Forest's pick, Nov 10): the 16 inch fan rides on a pod hinged across its own axis, so thrust passes through the hinge and a 20 g STS3032 only swings it. The pod is the thumb: it closes on a fixed finger, opens to 67 mm, and holds about 0.6 kg at half stall. 248 g at the tip; robot 18.91 kg, 16.9 min hover, T/W 1.34, better than the fixed bent claw (16.0 min, 1.29). Arm loads match the plain 16 inch fan (7075 idler pins on joints 1 to 4, fan within 60° of up). Standing on the folded finger makes walking worse in some rolls (joints 6 and 9 to 1.81 and 1.65 of stall) and needs the prop parked, so walking stays on the arm's underside.
  • Walking on the folded pod (Forest's pick, Nov 10): standing on the finger in the study's stance poses asks 1.44 and 1.24 of stall from joints 6 and 9. A torque-aware stance fixes it with no new parts: the arm arches up over its root and comes down nearly vertical onto the finger, so the load runs along the lower arm. With the hub 250 mm up, every joint stays at or under 0.33 of stall over the whole step (0.73 with twice the foot load). The prop parks itself: braked to a stop, then over the last 1.6° of the fold a 2-lobe collar on the prop adapter lands on a sprung post on the palm and turns the blades across, about 5 g per claw (16.9 min hover, T/W 1.34). The stopped motor's cogging still needs measuring.
  • Moves on the folded pod (Forest: "Yes, check the moves", Nov 10): one arm traced through a whole step at hub 250 mm (90 mm stance stroke, unload, 45 mm lift, swing, set-down, reload), with the straight servo moves between poses checked too. Sideways and diagonal steps are clean: the worst joint reaches 0.45 of stall (joints 6 / 9 at most 0.45 / 0.41, tip servos 11 / 12 at most 0.42 / 0.21), under the 0.67 limit. An in-out step centred on the stance runs 16 mm into the next arm at its inner end; moving the stroke 25 mm outward makes it clean at 0.41. The pod folds and unfolds only with the arm raised straight up (every joint 0.01, pod servo 0.41 at the park point, disc 33 mm clear of its own arm). A straight move up to that pose would hit 0.87 and brush the next arm, so the arm follows a planned path, pulling in low then standing up, peaking at 0.45. Two solver faults found on the way (arm coiling through itself, one blade swept into a neighbour mid-move) are now hard limits.

At 1 m every arm saturates in the 1 Hz wave test, so the dynamics factor is small for all of them and the order mostly follows the static score; the slower wave tests in the main-design table above are the fairer measure at this length. Score is the static composite (DOF, fluidity, loads, precision, mass, build effort) times the MuJoCo dynamics factor. Walk util is peak joint torque over capacity in claw-down stance (under 1 passes). Safe touchdown is the vertical speed the arm absorbs with fans holding hover weight; an unpowered 150 mm drop breaks every design.

Run it on Saturn

Code, catalog and CSVs are in the project folder under octopus-drone/joint-study/. On Saturn: pip install -r requirements.txt, then python3 run_study.py (12 workers by default), python3 rank.py and python3 build_page.py. Add JOINT_SCENARIO=tendon_optimistic for the low-friction case.
